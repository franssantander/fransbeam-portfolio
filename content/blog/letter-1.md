> "Some things are in our control and others not. Things in our control are opinion, pursuit, desire, aversion, and, in a word, whatever are our own actions. Things not in our control are body, property, reputation, command, and, in one word, whatever are not our own actions. The things in our control are by nature free, unrestrained, unhindered; but those not in our control are weak, slavish, restrained, belonging to others."
>
> — Epictetus

Many people think that caring about a goal means holding tightly to the outcome. But the Stoics taught something different: you can care deeply about the result without allowing it to control your peace.

The Stoic philosopher Antipater of Tarsus used the image of an archer to explain this. Imagine an archer preparing to take a shot. He checks his bow. He chooses the right arrow. He studies the wind, steadies his breathing, calms his nerves, and takes careful aim. Everything up to that moment is his responsibility.

But once the arrow leaves the string, it is no longer completely in his hands. The wind may suddenly change. The target may move. The weather may affect the arrow's path. Something unexpected may happen. The archer can control how well he prepares and how carefully he takes the shot. He cannot control everything that happens after he releases it. That does not mean he does not want to hit the target. Of course he does.

Stoicism is not about becoming indifferent to results. It is about placing your sense of success in something more reliable: **how well you handled what was within your control.** Before an important meeting, a difficult conversation, a job application, a date, a presentation, or any moment that matters, remind yourself of two things.

What belongs to you is your preparation, effort, intention, honesty, attitude, timing, boundaries, and the way you respond. What does not fully belong to you is whether someone agrees with you, likes you, chooses you, buys from you, stays with you, promotes you, or whether circumstances go your way.

Then make one promise to yourself:

**Judge yourself by what was yours to control.**

Instead of asking only,

_Did I get what I wanted?_

Ask:

_Did I prepare well?_

_Did I say what needed to be said?_

_Did I act with character under pressure?_

_Did I give the effort I was capable of giving?_

_What can I do better next time?_

This keeps your attention where it is useful.

On improving instead of overthinking.

On adjusting instead of complaining.

On learning instead of blaming.

And even when things go well, it reminds you not to become careless or arrogant.

Do everything you can before you release the arrow. Prepare well. Aim carefully.

Give your best effort.

Then let it fly.

Accept that the world will also have its say in where it lands.

This applies to your career, relationships, family, ambitions, and almost anything worth pursuing.

You are responsible for the shot.

Not the wind.
